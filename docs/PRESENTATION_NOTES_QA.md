# Presentation Notes and Q&A

Use these notes alongside [Freight_Signal_Capstone.pptx](Freight_Signal_Capstone.pptx).
The deck is designed for an approximately 8-10 minute presentation followed
by questions.

## Suggested talk track

### Slide 1 — Freight Signal
Introduce the project as a delivery-risk decision-support prototype. State
early that the included data is synthetic, so the work demonstrates a method
and application rather than validated real-world prediction.

### Slide 2 — The planning problem
Explain the practical need: identify orders worth checking before a delay
occurs. Describe planners as the users and emphasize that a model output is a
review signal, not an automatic operational decision.

### Slide 3 — What the dataset contains
Walk through the 438 rows, 16 columns, 15 potential inputs, and balanced
219/219 target classes. Mention supplier, route, order, and logistics fields.
Clarify that generated labels are not observed delivery outcomes.

### Slide 4 — Data quality and EDA
Report that the checked-in sample has no missing values or exact duplicates.
IQR outliers are flagged for review rather than automatically removed. Call
out the strongest numeric associations, especially supplier reliability
(-0.388) and historical delay rate (+0.339), and say explicitly that
correlation does not imply causation.

### Slide 5 — Leakage-safe preparation
Explain the stratified 80/20 split (350 train, 88 test). Identifiers are
excluded. Missing-value statistics, one-hot categories, and scaling are fit
using training data only, then reused for test and future inputs. Cross-
validation folds also fit their own preprocessing.

### Slide 6 — Model selection
The optional tuning search compares decision trees, RBF SVC, and gradient
boosting. The checked-in run selected an RBF SVC with C=0.1 and mean
five-fold training accuracy of 76.3%. Do not imply this is the universally
best model; it is the selection for this particular synthetic split/search.

### Slide 7 — Test results
Present 78.4% accuracy, 78.4% balanced accuracy, and 0.835 ROC-AUC. The
high-risk class has 77.8% precision and 79.5% recall. The confusion matrix
contains 9 missed high-risk examples and 10 false alarms. Compare to the 50%
majority baseline, noting that the synthetic dataset is balanced.

### Slide 8 — What the errors mean
Explain the trade-off: increasing high-risk recall may also increase the
number of false alarms and planner workload. The operational threshold should
be chosen with stakeholders after testing against real outcomes.

### Slide 9 — Application and demo
Show the Flask interface, enter one shipment, and explain the risk label and
probability. If time permits, show batch file scoring. Mention the JSON API
and `/health` endpoint. A risk probability is not necessarily calibrated.

### Slide 10 — Recommendations
Recommend a limited, human-reviewed pilot only after replacing synthetic
examples with approved historical data. Define the late-delivery label,
validate over time and by operating segment, agree on an alert threshold, and
monitor model performance and planner outcomes.

### Slide 11 — Limitations and next steps
Reiterate synthetic labels, small dataset, reused holdout, and lack of
calibration/fairness/causal evaluation. The next milestone is acquiring
representative history and a temporal validation set.

### Slide 12 — Q&A
Close with the message that this is a reproducible technical prototype, not
an autonomous logistics decision-maker.

## Likely evaluation questions

### Why is delivery risk the target?
It frames a practical prioritization question: which orders should a planner
review? In a real deployment the target must be defined using observed
arrival-versus-promise records and an agreed delay tolerance.

### Where did the data come from?
The checked-in data is generated by the repository's synthetic sample-data
generator. It is not a real company dataset, and the target is derived from a
weighted risk formula plus noise before class balancing.

### What does a high-risk prediction mean?
It means the current classifier predicts class 1 for the supplied feature
record. It is a triage prompt for a human, not proof that a shipment will be
late.

### How did you prevent leakage?
The raw records are split before preprocessing is fitted. Imputation,
categorical encoding, and numeric scaling use training data only. During
cross-validation, each fold gets independently fitted preprocessing. The
order identifier is excluded.

### Why report multiple metrics?
Accuracy is an overall summary; recall measures detection of high-risk cases;
precision quantifies the reliability of alerts; F1 balances precision and
recall; balanced accuracy treats class recalls evenly; ROC-AUC measures
ranking over thresholds. No single metric captures operational cost.

### What does the confusion matrix tell us?
On the 88-record test split there were 34 true low-risk predictions, 10 false
alarms, 9 missed high-risk records, and 35 correctly detected high-risk
records. The costs of the two error types must be supplied by the business.

### Why use an SVC?
The optional grid search compared several model families using stratified
five-fold accuracy on the training subset. The stored tuned result selected
an RBF SVC with C=0.1. This is a result of the current sample and search
space, not a general claim that SVC is always best.

### Is 78.4% accuracy good enough to deploy?
That cannot be concluded from this experiment. The dataset is synthetic and
small, and the same held-out split has been reused during iteration. Real,
representative and preferably temporal validation is required before an
operational decision.

### Are the displayed risk probabilities calibrated?
Calibration has not been measured. ROC-AUC evaluates ranking, not whether a
reported probability matches the observed frequency. Calibration curves and
proper scoring rules should be assessed on representative validation data.

### What happens if the business needs fewer missed delays?
A threshold can be selected to prioritize recall, but this can increase false
alarms. Compare candidate thresholds on real validation data and quantify
planner workload and delay costs with stakeholders.

### How would you monitor this after deployment?
Track input quality, score distributions, alert volumes, calibration, and
precision/recall when labels become available. Break results down by time,
route, supplier, and shipping mode; investigate drift and uneven error rates.

### What is the most important next step?
Replace synthetic examples with approved historical shipment records, define
the target and prediction-time feature rules, then validate on a later time
period that was not used for model development.
